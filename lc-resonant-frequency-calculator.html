Formula

f₀ = 1 / (2π √(L C))    ω₀ = 1/√(LC)    Z₀ = √(L/C)
series RLC: Q = (1/R) √(L/C), bandwidth = f₀ / Q    RC corner f_c = 1 / (2π R C)

Worked example

L = 10 µH, C = 100 pF: √(LC) = √(10⁻¹⁵) = 3.162 × 10⁻⁸ s, so f₀ = 1/(2π × 3.162 × 10⁻⁸) = 5.033 MHz. With R = 10 Ω in series, Z₀ = 316.2 Ω and Q = 31.6, giving a 159 kHz bandwidth.

How it works

An inductor and capacitor exchange energy at the resonant frequency f₀, the basis of RF filters, oscillators, matching networks and the drive circuits of electro-optic modulators and photodiode front-ends. The series resistance sets how sharp the resonance is (Q).

Real components add parasitic capacitance and resistance, so measured resonances usually sit a little lower than the ideal value.

FAQ

What is the resonant frequency of 1 µH and 1 nF?

About 5.03 MHz.

Does series or parallel LC change f₀?

No, the ideal f₀ is the same; the Q formulas differ.

What does the RC corner mean here?

It's the −3 dB frequency of an RC low-pass made from the same R and C, included for convenience.
