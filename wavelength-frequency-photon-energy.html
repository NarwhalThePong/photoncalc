Formula

f = c / λ    E = h f = h c / λ    ν̃ = 1 / λ
E[eV] ≈ 1239.842 / λ[nm]    f[THz] ≈ 299 792.458 / λ[nm]

Worked example

Telecom C-band light at λ = 1550 nm: f = 299 792 458 / 1.55×10⁻⁶ = 193.41 THz, and E = 1239.842 / 1550 = 0.7999 eV. Its wavenumber is 1 / (1.55×10⁻⁴ cm) = 6451.6 cm⁻¹.

How it works

Wavelength, frequency, photon energy and wavenumber are four ways of describing the same light. Laser datasheets quote wavelength, spectroscopists use wavenumbers, semiconductor physicists compare photon energy with a band gap, and telecom engineers lay out channels on a frequency grid in THz. The calculator uses the exact SI values of c, h and the elementary charge, so the conversion error is just rounding.

All wavelengths here are vacuum wavelengths. Inside a material of index n the wavelength shrinks to λ/n, but frequency and photon energy do not change.

FAQ

How do I convert nm to eV?

Divide 1239.84 by the wavelength in nanometres. For example 532 nm → 2.33 eV. The constant is hc/e expressed in eV·nm.

What frequency is 1550 nm?

About 193.4 THz. The ITU DWDM grid is anchored at 193.1 THz (1552.52 nm).

Is the wavelength measured in air or vacuum?

This tool uses vacuum wavelengths. Air wavelengths are smaller by the refractive index of air (about 1.00027 in the visible).
