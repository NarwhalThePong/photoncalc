Formula

R = η q λ / (h c) ≈ η · λ[nm] / 1239.84  A/W
I = R P    V = I R_L    i_shot = √(2 q I)  A/√Hz    photon rate = P λ / (h c)

Worked example

An InGaAs photodiode with 80 % quantum efficiency at 1550 nm: R = 0.8 × 1550 / 1239.84 = 1.000 A/W (an ideal detector would give 1.250 A/W). With 1 mW incident it produces 1.00 mA, which is 50 mV across a 50 Ω load, with a shot-noise floor of 17.9 pA/√Hz.

How it works

Each absorbed photon frees at most one electron, so a detector's current per watt rises linearly with wavelength until the photon energy falls below the band gap and the responsivity collapses. Quantum efficiency captures reflection and incomplete absorption.

Shot noise is the unavoidable noise of counting discrete electrons. Multiply the density by √(bandwidth) to get the rms noise current. Real receivers also have dark current and amplifier noise, which this calculator does not include.

FAQ

What responsivity do silicon photodiodes have?

Peak values near 0.5–0.6 A/W around 850–950 nm, falling off past about 1000 nm.

Why can't R exceed λ/1239.84?

That is 100 % quantum efficiency; only avalanche or other gain devices beat it, and they quote gain separately.

How do I get the noise in my bandwidth?

Multiply the shot-noise density by the square root of the bandwidth in Hz, e.g. 17.9 pA/√Hz × √(1 GHz) ≈ 0.57 µA rms.
