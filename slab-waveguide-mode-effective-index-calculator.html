Formula

k₀ d √(n_f² − N²) = mπ + arctan(rs γs/κ) + arctan(rc γc/κ),   κ = k₀√(n_f² − N²),   γs,c = k₀√(N² − ns,c²)
TE: r = 1;   TM: rs = n_f²/n_s², rc = n_f²/n_c²    (k₀ = 2π/λ, m = 0, 1, 2 …)
V = k₀ d √(n_f² − n_s²),   aTE = (n_s² − n_c²)/(n_f² − n_s²),   aTM = aTE n_f⁴/n_c⁴,   b = (N² − n_s²)/(n_f² − n_s²)
mode m is guided when V > mπ + arctan√a,   number of modes = ⌊(V − arctan√a)/π⌋ + 1,   cutoff thickness dm = (mπ + arctan√a) / (k₀√(n_f² − n_s²))

Worked example

A 220 nm silicon film (n = 3.476) between silica claddings (n = 1.444) at 1550 nm has V = 2.820 and supports one TE and one TM mode, because a symmetric slab only gains its second mode at V = π, a thickness of 245 nm. The TE₀ mode has N = 2.8478 (b = 0.603) with 81.0 % of its power in the silicon and a 1/e field tail of 100.5 nm into each cladding; the TM₀ mode is much weaker guided, N = 2.0533 with 58.4 % confinement and a 169 nm tail. Remove the upper oxide (air cover) and the indices drop to 2.8309 (TE₀) and 1.8908 (TM₀); the slab now also has a TE₀ cutoff, at 24.8 nm, and stays single-mode for TE up to 270 nm. For a weakly guiding glass film, n = 1.50 on n = 1.45 with air above at 1550 nm, the asymmetry is large (a = 7.47 for TE): nothing is guided below 0.784 µm, and a 2 µm film carries one TE mode (N = 1.47651, 86.1 % confinement) and one TM mode until the TE₁ mode appears at 2.80 µm.

How it works

The slab, a high-index film between two lower-index layers, is the simplest optical waveguide and the starting point for almost every integrated photonics design. Light is trapped in the film by total internal reflection, but only at discrete angles where the round trip adds up to a whole number of 2π including the phase picked up at each reflection. Each solution is a mode, described by its effective index N = β/k₀: the mode travels as if it were a plane wave in a material of index N, which always lies between the higher cladding index and the core index. The effective index sets the phase velocity, the length of couplers and resonators and the period of gratings and poling, so it is the first number you need.

This calculator solves the exact transcendental equation for every guided TE mode (electric field parallel to the film) and TM mode (magnetic field parallel to it) by bisection. It gives the normalized frequency V and asymmetry a used in Kogelnik's universal b–V charts, the number of modes, the thickness at which TE₀/TM₀ and TE₁/TM₁ appear (the second is the single-mode limit), and for each mode the fraction of power inside the core and in each cladding plus how far the evanescent field reaches. Confinement matters for modulators, lasers and nonlinear devices, whose overlap with the active core it sets; the tail length tells you how thick a cladding must be to avoid leakage into a silicon substrate and how close two waveguides must be to couple.

Limits: the slab is infinitely wide, so it gives the vertical problem only. A real rib or strip waveguide has a lower effective index; the effective index method (solve the vertical slab, then a lateral slab using the slab indices) gets close for wide guides, and the TFLN poling calculator on this site does exactly that for lithium niobate ridges. Materials are lossless and isotropic, with the indices taken at the wavelength you enter (use the Sellmeier calculator for dispersion), and the substrate must be the higher of the two cladding indices. Leaky and radiation modes are not computed. The confinement factor is the fraction of the guided power flux in the core, which is the usual definition for passive and electro-optic devices; for gain in semiconductor lasers some authors use the electric-field energy fraction instead, which differs for TM modes.

FAQ

How do I calculate the effective index of a slab waveguide?

Solve k₀d√(n_f² − N²) = mπ + arctan(γ_s/κ) + arctan(γ_c/κ) for N, with κ = k₀√(n_f² − N²) and γ = k₀√(N² − n²) in each cladding (for TM multiply each γ/κ by n_f²/n²). There is no closed form, so it is solved numerically; this calculator does it for every mode. A 220 nm silicon slab in silica at 1550 nm gives N = 2.848 for TE₀ and 2.053 for TM₀.

How many modes does a slab waveguide support?

⌊(V − arctan√a)/π⌋ + 1, where V = k₀d√(n_f² − n_s²) and a is the asymmetry. A symmetric slab (a = 0) always guides TE₀ and becomes multimode at V = π; an asymmetric one has no guided mode at all below V = arctan√a.

What is the single-mode thickness of a silicon slab?

With silica on both sides at 1550 nm, about 245 nm: the TE₁ and TM₁ modes appear at V = π, d = λ / (2√(n_f² − n_s²)) = 1.55 / (2 × 3.162) µm. With air on top the TE₁ limit moves to about 270 nm. Standard 220 nm SOI sits just below this limit.

What is the difference between TE and TM modes in a slab?

TE modes have the electric field in the plane of the film; TM modes have the magnetic field there and the electric field mostly perpendicular, so they see the index step directly and are guided less strongly. In high-contrast films such as silicon the TM effective index is far lower (2.05 vs 2.85 for 220 nm SOI) and its field extends much further into the claddings.

What is the confinement factor?

The fraction of the mode's power that flows inside the core. It scales the gain of a laser, the phase shift of a modulator and the overlap of a nonlinear interaction. In 220 nm SOI it is 81 % for TE₀ and 58 % for TM₀; in a 3 µm film of n = 1.54 on n = 1.515 glass at 633 nm, 98.8 % for TE₀.

What are V number, b and asymmetry a?

The normalized frequency V = k₀d√(n_f² − n_s²), the normalized propagation constant b = (N² − n_s²)/(n_f² − n_s²) between 0 (cutoff) and 1, and the asymmetry a = (n_s² − n_c²)/(n_f² − n_s²). With these three numbers every slab maps onto Kogelnik's universal b–V curves.

How far does the evanescent field extend?

The field falls as exp(−γx) in each cladding, so 1/γ = λ / (2π√(N² − n²)) is the 1/e depth of the field (the intensity decays twice as fast). For TE₀ in 220 nm SOI it is about 100 nm, which is why a 2–3 µm buried oxide is enough to isolate the mode from the silicon substrate.
