Formula

V = (2π a / λ) · NA    single-mode if V < 2.405 (first zero of J₀)
λ_c = 2π a · NA / 2.405    modes ≈ V² / 2 (large V)
Marcuse: w / a ≈ 0.65 + 1.619 V^(−3/2) + 2.879 V^(−6)

Worked example

a = 4.2 µm, NA = 0.14: λ_c = 2π·4200·0.14/2.4048 = 1536.3 nm. At 1550 nm, V = 2.384 (single-mode); at 1310 nm, V = 2.820 (two-mode in theory).

How it works

The V-number combines core size, index contrast and wavelength into a single number that decides how many modes a step-index fiber guides. Below V = 2.405 only the fundamental LP01 mode propagates; above it LP11 appears, then more modes roughly as V²/2.

Real fiber datasheets quote a measured (and, in cable, a shorter cabled) cutoff wavelength, because bends strip the weakly guided LP11 mode. Treat this theoretical cutoff as an upper bound.

FAQ

Why 2.405?

It is the first zero of the Bessel function J₀, where the LP11 mode stops being cut off.

Does it work for graded-index fiber?

Not exactly; the 2.405 rule and V²/2 count are for step-index profiles.

What V do designers aim for?

Usually 2.0–2.4 at the operating wavelength, to stay single-mode with margin.
