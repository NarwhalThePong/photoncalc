PPLN phase-matching temperature calculator (SHG)

Formula

Find T such that Λ_required(λp, T) = Λ_grating, where
Λ_required = m / ( n_e(λp/2, T)/(λp/2) − 2 n_e(λp, T)/λp )   (solved by bisection over 0–250 °C)

Worked example

A 6.96 µm grating pumped at 1064 nm phase-matches at about 33.3 °C by this model, in line with Covesion's MSHG1064 crystal data (6.96 µm near 33 °C). A 19.3 µm grating with a 1550 nm pump matches at about 52 °C.

How it works

Commercial PPLN crystals carry several fixed gratings, and the last knob is the oven temperature. This tool inverts the poling-period calculation: it searches the temperature at which the required period equals the grating you have. It also reports how quickly the required period moves with temperature, which tells you how sensitive the match is.

If no temperature between 0 and 250 °C works, the tool shows the required period at both ends so you can see which grating to pick instead. Real crystals can be off by a few °C because of fabrication tolerance in the period and the MgO content.

FAQ

Why does my crystal phase-match at a different temperature?

Poling-period tolerances, MgO concentration and oven calibration each shift the match by a few °C. Scan around the predicted temperature.

Is there a temperature limit?

MgO:PPLN is usually run from room temperature to about 200 °C; undoped PPLN needs over ~100 °C to avoid photorefractive damage.

Does it work for waveguides?

No, thin-film and diffused waveguides need effective indices from a mode solver.
