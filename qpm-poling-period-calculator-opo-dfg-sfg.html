Formula

1/λᵢ = 1/λp − 1/λs (energy conservation)
Δk = 2π [ n_e(λp)/λp − n_e(λs)/λs − n_e(λᵢ)/λᵢ ] − 2π m/Λ = 0
⇒ Λ = m / ( n_e(λp)/λp − n_e(λs)/λs − n_e(λᵢ)/λᵢ )   (type-0, all extraordinary)

Worked example

A 1064 nm pumped OPO at degeneracy (signal = idler = 2128 nm) at 40 °C: n_e(1.064) = 2.1524 and n_e(2.128) = 2.1195, so Λ = 1 / (2.1524/1.064 − 2 × 2.1195/2.128) ≈ 32.3 µm. A 1550 nm signal from the same pump at 100 °C gives an idler at 3393 nm and Λ ≈ 30.3 µm.

How it works

Every χ⁽²⁾ process mixes three waves whose photon energies add up: in an optical parametric oscillator (OPO) or difference-frequency generation (DFG) a pump photon splits into a signal and an idler; sum-frequency generation (SFG) runs the same process backwards. The same quasi-phase-matching condition applies to all of them, so one calculator covers OPO, OPA, DFG and SFG. Enter the highest-frequency wave as the pump.

The indices come from the Gayer et al. temperature-dependent Sellmeier equation for 5 mol% MgO:congruent LiNbO₃, valid from roughly 0.5 to 4 µm. Results near the edge of that range (idlers past ~4 µm) are extrapolations and should be checked against the crystal vendor's tuning curves.

FAQ

Which wavelength is the pump?

The shortest wavelength (highest photon energy). For DFG, enter the shorter of your two input lasers as the pump and the longer as the signal.

Why does the period change with temperature?

The extraordinary index of LN depends on temperature differently at each wavelength, so heating the crystal re-tunes the phase matching. OPOs often use this to tune the signal and idler.

Does this include the thermal expansion of the grating?

No. Thermal expansion changes the period by well under 0.1 % over 100 °C, smaller than the Sellmeier uncertainty.
