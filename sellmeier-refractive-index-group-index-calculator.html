Formula

n² = 1 + Σ Bᵢ λ² / (λ² − Cᵢ)   (λ in µm)
n_g = n − λ dn/dλ    β₂ = λ³ / (2π c²) · d²n/dλ²    D = −(λ / c) · d²n/dλ²

Worked example

Fused silica at 1550 nm: n = 1.44402, n_g = 1.46260, β₂ = −27.9 fs²/mm and D = +21.9 ps/(nm·km), the familiar anomalous dispersion of bulk silica. N-BK7 at the helium d-line (587.6 nm) gives n = 1.51680, matching its catalog n_d.

How it works

A Sellmeier equation is an empirical fit of a material's refractive index versus wavelength. From it you also get the group index (which sets pulse travel time and cavity FSR) and the group-velocity dispersion (which stretches ultrashort pulses). The derivatives here are taken numerically from the fit.

Each fit is only valid over the wavelength range it was measured in, which the calculator enforces. Silicon's fit starts at 1.36 µm because silicon absorbs strongly below its band gap.

FAQ

What is the refractive index of fused silica at 1550 nm?

About 1.444 (Malitson fit), with a group index near 1.463.

Why does silica have zero dispersion near 1.27 µm?

Material dispersion d²n/dλ² changes sign there; in fiber, waveguide dispersion shifts the zero to about 1.31 µm.

What sign convention is used for GVD?

β₂ > 0 is normal dispersion (red travels faster); D has the opposite sign.
