Formula

E = P_avg / f_rep    P_peak = k · E / τ    duty = τ · f_rep
Gaussian beam, 1/e² radius w: F₀ = 2E / (π w²), I₀ = 2 P_peak / (π w²)
photons = E λ / (h c)

Worked example

1 W at 1 kHz with 10 ns Gaussian pulses: E = 1 mJ and P_peak = 0.9394 × 1 mJ / 10 ns = 93.94 kW. Focused to a 100 µm diameter spot: F₀ = 2 × 1 mJ / (π × (50 µm)²) = 25.46 J/cm², I₀ = 2.39 × 10⁹ W/cm². At 1064 nm that is 5.36 × 10¹⁵ photons per pulse.

How it works

Peak power is not just energy divided by pulse width: smooth pulses put some energy in their wings, so the peak is lower by a shape factor k (0.9394 for Gaussian, 0.8814 for sech² when τ is the FWHM). Likewise, the on-axis fluence of a Gaussian beam is twice the energy divided by the 1/e² area.

Damage thresholds of optics and ablation thresholds of materials are usually quoted as peak fluence (J/cm²) for ns pulses and peak intensity (W/cm²) for ultrafast pulses.

FAQ

Why is peak fluence 2E/(πw²)?

A Gaussian beam's centre is twice as intense as the average over its 1/e² circle.

Which pulse shape should I pick?

Q-switched ns pulses: Gaussian. Soliton mode-locked fs/ps pulses: sech².

My autocorrelator gives the width. Can I use it?

Divide by 1.414 (Gaussian) or 1.543 (sech²) first.
