Formula

sin θ₂ = sin θ₁ / nr,   θ₃ = A − θ₂,   sin θ₄ = nr sin θ₃,   δ = θ₁ + θ₄ − A    (nr = n / n₀)
δmin = 2 arcsin(nr sin(A/2)) − A    nr = sin((A + δmin)/2) / sin(A/2)
light passes only if A < 2θc,   θc = arcsin(1/nr)    thin prism: δ ≈ (nr − 1) A
dδ/dn = 2 sin(A/2) / (n₀ cos θ₁) at minimum deviation    nF − nC = (nd − 1) / Vd

Worked example

An equilateral (A = 60°) N-BK7 prism with nd = 1.5168 has a minimum deviation of 2 arcsin(1.5168 × sin 30°) − 60° = 38.65°, reached when the ray enters and leaves at 49.32° and runs parallel to the base inside. A ray entering at 45° instead is deviated by slightly more; with n = 1.5 it is 37.38° against the 37.18° minimum. Below about 29.19° incidence the ray hits the second face beyond the 41.25° critical angle and is totally internally reflected, and no ray at all gets through a BK7 prism with an apex above 82.49°. With the Abbe number 64.17 the F-to-C index spread is 0.5168 / 64.17 = 0.00805, which fans blue (486 nm) and red (656 nm) light apart by 0.708° (12.4 mrad). Going the other way, if you measure δmin = 38.00° on a 60° prism, the glass index is sin 49° / sin 30° = 1.50942; δmin = 60° means n = √3 = 1.7321.

How it works

A prism bends light because each face refracts it by Snell's law, and the two bends add. How much the ray is deviated depends on the angle it comes in at: as the incidence angle increases, the deviation first falls, passes through a minimum, then rises again. The minimum happens when the ray passes symmetrically, entering and leaving at the same angle, with the internal ray parallel to the base. At that point the deviation depends only on the apex angle and the index, which is why measuring the minimum deviation on a spectrometer is the classic precision method for finding the refractive index of glass, liquids (in a hollow prism) and crystals.

The calculator works either way: from the index to the minimum deviation, or from a measured minimum deviation back to the index. It also traces a single ray at any incidence angle, reporting each internal angle and whether it is trapped by total internal reflection at the second face, and gives the two hard limits: the largest apex angle that can pass light at all (twice the critical angle) and the smallest incidence angle at which the ray still gets out. Because the index changes with wavelength, a prism spreads white light into a spectrum. The angular dispersion dδ/dn at minimum deviation, multiplied by the F-to-C index difference (from the Abbe number), gives how far apart blue and red end up. For an index at another wavelength, see the Sellmeier calculator.

Limits: a single prism in one plane (rays in the principal section), isotropic material, and geometric optics only. Reflection losses at the faces (see the Snell's law and Fresnel calculator) and the dependence of n on wavelength beyond the Abbe-number estimate are not included. Birefringent prisms such as calcite or quartz have two indices; enter the one for the polarization you use.

FAQ

What is the formula for minimum deviation of a prism?

δ_min = 2 arcsin(n sin(A/2)) − A for a prism of index n and apex angle A in air. In a medium of index n₀, replace n by n/n₀. For n = 1.5 and A = 60°, δ_min = 37.18°.

How do I find the refractive index from the angle of minimum deviation?

n = sin((A + δ_min)/2) / sin(A/2). For a 60° prism with δ_min = 38°, n = sin 49° / sin 30° = 1.5094. Choose the measured-δ_min mode above.

Why does minimum deviation happen at the symmetric path?

The deviation as a function of incidence angle has a single minimum. If it occurred at an asymmetric path, reversing the ray (which follows the same path backwards) would give a second minimum, so the minimum must be the symmetric case where the entry and exit angles are equal.

When does a ray not come out of a prism?

When it meets the second face at more than the critical angle arcsin(1/n) it is totally internally reflected. For any incidence this always happens if the apex angle exceeds twice the critical angle: 82.5° for BK7 and about 33.4° for silicon (n ≈ 3.48).

How much does a prism disperse white light?

At minimum deviation, Δδ = 2 sin(A/2) / cos θ₁ × Δn. For a 60° N-BK7 prism (Abbe number 64.17) blue F light and red C light are separated by about 0.71°. Dense flint glasses with lower Abbe numbers spread light several times more.

What is the thin-prism approximation?

For small apex angles and near-normal incidence, δ ≈ (n − 1)A. A 10° fused-silica wedge (n ≈ 1.444) deviates light by about 4.44° by this rule; the exact minimum is 4.46°.
