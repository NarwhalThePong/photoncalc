Formula

Δk = 2π n_e(λ_SH)/λ_SH − 2 · 2π n_e(λ_p)/λ_p − 2π m/Λ = 0
⇒ Λ = m / ( n_e(λ_SH)/λ_SH − 2 n_e(λ_p)/λ_p ),   λ_SH = λ_p / 2
n_e² = a₁ + b₁f + (a₂ + b₂f)/(λ² − (a₃ + b₃f)²) + (a₄ + b₄f)/(λ² − a₅²) − a₆λ²,   f = (T − 24.5)(T + 570.82), λ in µm, T in °C

Worked example

1064 nm → 532 nm at 33 °C: n_e(1064) = 2.15046 and n_e(532) = 2.22689, so Λ = 1 / (2.22689/0.532 − 2×2.15046/1.064) = 6.96 µm. Covesion's MSHG1064 crystal lists a 6.96 µm grating phase-matched near 33 °C for a 1064 nm pump. For 1550 → 775 nm at 40 °C the period is about 19.3 µm.

How it works

In periodically poled lithium niobate (PPLN) the sign of the nonlinear coefficient d₃₃ is flipped every coherence length, so the second harmonic keeps growing instead of oscillating. The required period depends on the dispersion of the extraordinary index (all waves e-polarized, type-0), and therefore on wavelength and temperature; heating the crystal is the usual fine-tuning knob.

This calculator uses the Sellmeier coefficients of Gayer et al. for 5 mol% MgO-doped congruent LiNbO₃: a = (5.756, 0.0983, 0.2020, 189.32, 12.52, 1.32×10⁻²), b = (2.860×10⁻⁶, 4.700×10⁻⁸, 6.113×10⁻⁸, 1.516×10⁻⁴). It ignores thermal expansion of the period (a sub-0.1 % effect over 100 °C) and is a bulk-crystal model; thin-film LN waveguides need the waveguide's effective indices instead.

FAQ

What period do I need for 1550 nm SHG?

About 19.4 µm near room temperature in bulk MgO:PPLN by this model (19.34 µm at 40 °C, 18.9 µm at 150 °C); commercial 1550 nm SHG crystals offer several gratings around 19–20 µm so you can tune with temperature.

Why use 5% MgO-doped LN?

MgO doping raises photorefractive damage resistance, so the crystal can run near room temperature.

Does this work for thin-film lithium niobate (TFLN)?

Not directly. Waveguide dispersion shifts the period to a few µm for 1550 nm SHG; use effective indices from a mode solver instead.
