Formula

Link loss = L·α + Nc·Lc + Ns·Ls + other
Power budget = Ptx − Srx    Prx = Ptx − link loss
Margin = budget − link loss − safety margin    Lmax = (budget − connectors − splices − other − safety margin) / α

Worked example

A 40 km single-mode link at 1550 nm with 0.25 dB/km fiber loses 10 dB in the glass. Four connector pairs at 0.5 dB add 2 dB and six fusion splices at 0.1 dB add 0.6 dB, so the link loss is 12.6 dB. With a 0 dBm transmitter and a −24 dBm receiver the budget is 24 dB, the receiver sees about −12.6 dBm, and after a 3 dB safety allowance 8.4 dB of margin remains. The same optics would reach (24 − 2 − 0.6 − 3) / 0.25 = 73.6 km.

How it works

A loss budget checks, before anything is installed, that enough light reaches the receiver. The available budget is the difference between the weakest transmitter launch power and the receiver sensitivity from the transceiver datasheet. Every element in the path spends part of it: the fiber itself (attenuation times length), each mated connector pair and each splice, plus anything else in line such as passive splitters or WDM filters.

Designers keep a safety margin, often 3 dB, for fiber aging, temperature, dirty connectors and future repair splices. If the margin is negative the link is not expected to work; if the received power is above the receiver's overload limit you need an attenuator instead.

Limits: this is a power budget only. At high data rates or long distances, chromatic dispersion and other penalties can limit the link before loss does, and multimode links at 850 nm are often limited by modal bandwidth. Use datasheet worst-case values rather than typical ones, and after installation compare the estimate with an OTDR or a light-source and power-meter test.

FAQ

How do I calculate a fiber optic loss budget?

Multiply the fiber length by its attenuation, add the loss of each connector pair and splice, and add any other passive components. Compare that total with the transmitter power minus the receiver sensitivity, leaving a few dB of safety margin.

What loss values should I use for connectors and splices?

TIA-568 allows up to 0.75 dB per mated connector pair and 0.3 dB per splice. Good field work typically gives 0.3–0.5 dB per connector and under 0.1 dB per fusion splice, but budgets are usually written with the standard's maximums.

What is a typical fiber attenuation?

Standard single-mode fiber is about 0.35 dB/km at 1310 nm and 0.20–0.25 dB/km at 1550 nm; cabled specs allow up to 0.5 dB/km for outside plant and 1.0 dB/km for inside plant. OM3/OM4 multimode fiber is about 3 dB/km at 850 nm.

How much safety margin should a fiber link have?

Around 3 dB is common for single-mode links, covering aging, temperature and repair splices. Short multimode links sometimes use less, long-haul links often more.
