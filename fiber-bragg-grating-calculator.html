Formula

λB = 2 neff Λ    κ = π η Δn / λB    Rpeak = tanh²(κL)
Δλ0 = λB² / (π neff L) · √((κL)² + π²)    Δλ/ΔT ≈ λB(α + ξ)    Δλ/ε = λB(1 − pe)

Worked example

For a 1550 nm grating in SMF-28 (neff ≈ 1.447) the period is 1550 / (2 × 1.447) ≈ 535.6 nm. With Δn = 1 × 10⁻⁴ and η = 0.8, κ = π × 0.8 × 10⁻⁴ / 1550 nm ≈ 0.162 mm⁻¹, so a 10 mm grating has κL ≈ 1.62 and a peak reflectivity of tanh²(1.62) ≈ 85.5 %. Its bandwidth between the first zeros is about 0.187 nm. The Bragg wavelength moves by roughly 14 pm per kelvin and 1.2 pm per microstrain.

How it works

A fiber Bragg grating is a periodic change of the core's refractive index. Light whose wavelength satisfies the Bragg condition λB = 2neffΛ is reflected back by every period in phase, so the grating acts as a narrow reflector or, in transmission, a notch filter. The calculator uses coupled-mode theory for a uniform, unchirped, unapodized grating: the coupling coefficient κ sets how strongly the forward and backward modes exchange power, and κL sets the peak reflectivity.

Weak gratings (κL much less than 1) have a reflectivity close to (κL)² and a bandwidth set by the length, roughly λB²/(neffL). Strong gratings (κL above about 2) saturate near 100 % and the bandwidth is set by the index modulation instead, approaching ηΔn·λB/neff. Here Δn is the amplitude of the index modulation and η is the fraction of the mode's power that overlaps the written region.

The temperature and strain shifts use typical values for germanium-doped silica fiber: a thermal expansion α ≈ 0.55 × 10⁻⁶ /K, a thermo-optic coefficient ξ ≈ 8.6 × 10⁻⁶ /K and an effective photo-elastic constant pe ≈ 0.22. Real gratings vary with fiber type, coating and packaging, so calibrate sensors. The model also ignores the small shift of the centre wavelength caused by the average (dc) index change, apodization and chirp.

FAQ

How do I calculate the period of a fiber Bragg grating?

Λ = λ_B / (2 n_eff). For 1550 nm in standard single-mode fiber, with n_eff ≈ 1.447, that is about 536 nm. Phase masks for this have a period of 2Λ, about 1071 nm, because the first diffracted orders interfere to write the grating.

What index modulation do I need for 99 % reflectivity?

tanh²(κL) = 0.99 needs κL ≈ 2.99. For a 10 mm grating at 1550 nm with η = 0.8, κ = 0.299 mm⁻¹, which needs Δn ≈ 1.8 × 10⁻⁴.

How much does an FBG shift with temperature?

About 13–14 pm/K at 1550 nm for bare silica fiber, and about 12 pm/K at 1310 nm, since the shift scales with λ_B. Most of it comes from the thermo-optic effect rather than thermal expansion.

How much does an FBG shift with strain?

About 1.2 pm per microstrain at 1550 nm, from λ_B(1 − p_e) with p_e ≈ 0.22.
