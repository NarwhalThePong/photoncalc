Worked example

A 1 µJ, 300 fs Gaussian pulse at 1030 nm has a peak power of 3.13 MW. Focused to w = 100 µm in a 10 mm fused-silica window (n₂ = 2.74 × 10⁻²⁰ m²/W) the on-axis intensity is 19.9 GW/cm², the index rises by 5.5 × 10⁻⁶, and the B-integral is 0.333 rad: the spectrum broadens by only 4 % rms (±252 GHz of peak chirp, ±0.89 nm). The critical power for self-focusing is 4.03 MW, so this pulse is already at 0.78 P_cr and the plate acts as a Kerr lens of about 4.6 cm focal length; at 10 µJ (31 MW, 7.8 P_cr) the beam would collapse into a filament. In standard single-mode fiber at 1550 nm (A_eff = 80 µm², γ = 1.32 /(W·km)) a 1 ps sech² pulse of 58 pJ has 51.1 W peak power and a nonlinear length of 14.8 m; with β₂ = −21.7 ps²/km the dispersion length is also 14.8 m, so N = 0.999 and the pulse travels as a fundamental soliton (exact N = 1 at 51.2 W, soliton period 23.3 m). A silicon nitride waveguide (A_eff = 1 µm², γ = 0.97 /(W·m)) with 10 W peak power over 5 cm at 0.5 dB/cm (L_eff = 3.80 cm) accumulates 0.37 rad; a silicon wire (A_eff = 0.1 µm², γ = 162 /(W·m)) reaches 1.30 rad over 1 cm at only 1 W. A 200 W, 5 ps pulse at 1064 nm in 100 m of fiber with A_eff = 30 µm² gets φ = 101 rad: the spectrum splits into about 33 peaks and is 89 times wider (rms) than the input.

How it works

Every transparent material becomes slightly more refractive where light is intense: n = n₀ + n₂I, the optical Kerr effect. The change is tiny (n₂ of silica is 2.7 × 10⁻²⁰ m²/W, so even 10 GW/cm² raises the index by only 3 × 10⁻⁶), but over centimetres of glass or kilometres of fiber the extra phase adds up. The accumulated nonlinear phase at the peak of the pulse is the B-integral, B = (2π/λ)∫n₂I dz. It is the single number laser designers use to judge whether nonlinearity matters: below about 1 rad it is usually negligible, a few radians visibly broaden the spectrum and distort compressed pulses, and in high-power amplifiers it is kept below roughly 3 to 5 rad because small ripples on the beam grow by up to eB (small-scale self-focusing) and can destroy optics.

Two conventions are in use, and this calculator shows both. For a free-space Gaussian beam in bulk, the intensity that counts is the on-axis peak I₀ = 2P/(πw²), and B is quoted on axis. For a fiber or waveguide the mode keeps its shape, so the nonlinearity is written per watt, γ = 2πn₂/(λAeff), and the phase is φNL = γPLeff, with the nonlinear length LNL = 1/(γP) being the distance for 1 rad. A Gaussian beam has Aeff = πw², so its mode-averaged phase is exactly half the on-axis B; mixing the two up is a common factor-of-two error. Loss is handled with the effective length Leff = (1 − e−αL)/α, which saturates at 1/α (21.7 km for 0.2 dB/km fiber).

In time, the nonlinear phase follows the pulse shape, so the leading edge is red-shifted and the trailing edge blue-shifted: this is self-phase modulation (SPM). For an unchirped pulse with no dispersion the peak frequency excursion, the rms spectral broadening and the number of spectral peaks follow from φ alone (Agrawal, chapter 4); the calculator uses the exact closed forms for Gaussian and sech² pulses. If you enter β₂, it also gives the dispersion length and the soliton order N = √(LD/LNL): in anomalous dispersion (β₂ < 0) a sech² pulse with N = 1 keeps its shape because SPM and dispersion cancel, which is why the SPM numbers are only an upper estimate whenever the medium is longer than about a third of LD. In space, the same index bump acts as a positive lens. Above the critical power Pcr, which depends only on λ, n₀ and n₂ and not on beam size, a beam in bulk self-focuses to collapse; for a Gaussian beam Fibich and Gaeta found Pcr = 1.8962 λ²/(4πn₀n₂), 1.8 % above the Townes-soliton lower bound. For fused silica near 1 µm this is about 4 MW, which is why single-mode fiber amplifiers top out at a few MW of peak power.

Limits: n₂ depends on wavelength, polarization and pulse duration (for pulses longer than about 1 ps, delayed Raman (nuclear) and electrostrictive contributions add roughly 15–25 % in silica), so the presets are typical literature values and published numbers for the same material scatter by 10–30 %. The model is the instantaneous Kerr effect only: no two-photon or multiphoton absorption (important in silicon above about 1 GW/cm², where it limits the phase you can reach), free carriers, plasma, Raman self-frequency shift, self-steepening, thermal lensing or photorefraction. The B-integral uses a constant beam size along the medium (no diffraction or focusing inside the sample; for a focused beam integrate segment by segment), and the Kerr-lens focal length is the thin-sample parabolic estimate, valid only when L ≪ f and P ≪ Pcr. The self-focusing collapse distance and filamentation dynamics are not computed. SPM numbers assume an unchirped input and no dispersion within the medium.

FAQ

What is the B-integral?

The nonlinear phase accumulated at the peak of a pulse through the Kerr effect, B = (2π/λ)∫n₂I(z)dz, in radians. For a constant intensity it is k₀n₂IL. A 300 fs, 1 µJ pulse focused to w = 100 µm in 10 mm of fused silica at 1030 nm has B = 0.33 rad on axis; amplifier designers usually keep the total B of a system below about 3 to 5 rad.

How do I calculate the nonlinear coefficient γ of a fiber?

γ = 2πn₂/(λA_eff). Standard single-mode fiber at 1550 nm with n₂ = 2.6 × 10⁻²⁰ m²/W and A_eff = 80 µm² has γ = 1.32 /(W·km); a silicon nitride waveguide with A_eff = 1 µm² has about 0.97 /(W·m), and a silicon nanowire around 160 /(W·m). The nonlinear phase is then γPL_eff.

What is the critical power for self-focusing?

The peak power above which a beam in a bulk Kerr medium focuses itself to collapse, independent of beam size: P_cr = 1.8962 λ²/(4πn₀n₂) for a Gaussian beam (Fibich & Gaeta 2000), about 4.0 MW for fused silica at 1030 nm and roughly 9 to 10 MW at 1550 nm. The often-quoted 0.148 λ²/(n₀n₂) is the slightly lower Townes-soliton bound, and Marburger's 3.77 λ²/(8πn₀n₂) lies between the two.

What is the nonlinear length L_NL?

L_NL = 1/(γP₀), the distance over which the peak acquires 1 rad of nonlinear phase. Compare it with the dispersion length L_D = T₀²/|β₂|: if L_NL is much shorter than L_D, SPM dominates; if they are equal in anomalous dispersion, a sech² pulse is a fundamental soliton (N = 1).

How much does self-phase modulation broaden the spectrum?

For an unchirped Gaussian pulse with no dispersion, the rms spectral width grows by √(1 + 0.770 φ²) and the spectrum develops about φ/π + ½ peaks. At φ = 0.33 rad that is only 4 %; at 101 rad (200 W, 5 ps in 100 m of 30 µm² fiber) it is 89 times wider with about 33 peaks. The largest frequency shift is ±0.858 φ/T₀ for a Gaussian.

Is the B-integral the same as γPL?

Not for a free-space beam. γPL_eff with A_eff = πw² is the mode-averaged phase, which is exactly half the on-axis B of a Gaussian beam. Fiber papers use γPL_eff; bulk amplifier papers quote the on-axis B. For a guided mode the calculator uses I = P/A_eff, so the two are the same.

Why does silicon show less nonlinear phase than its n₂ suggests?

Silicon has two-photon absorption (β ≈ 0.8 cm/GW at 1.54 µm, Dinu et al. 2003), which removes power and generates free carriers at the same intensities where the Kerr phase becomes useful. This calculator ignores it, so for silicon above about 1 GW/cm² treat the phase as an upper bound. Silicon nitride and silica have no two-photon absorption at 1550 nm.
