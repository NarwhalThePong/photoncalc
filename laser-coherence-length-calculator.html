Formula

Δν = c Δλ / λ²    Lc = c τc / n
Lorentzian: τc = 1/(πΔν), visibility V = exp(−πΔν·nΔL/c)    Gaussian: τc = √(2 ln 2/π)/Δν ≈ 0.664/Δν, V = exp(−(πΔν·nΔL/c)²/(4 ln 2))
Rule of thumb: Lc ≈ c/(nΔν) ≈ λ²/(nΔλ)

Worked example

A 1550 nm fiber laser with a 100 kHz Lorentzian linewidth has τc = 1/(π × 100 kHz) ≈ 3.18 µs, so Lc ≈ 954 m in air, or about 650 m inside SMF-28 (group index 1.468). A 1 nm-wide 1550 nm source (Δν ≈ 125 GHz) has a rule-of-thumb coherence length λ²/Δλ ≈ 2.4 mm, which is why broadband sources are used for low-coherence interferometry and OCT.

How it works

The coherence time τc is how long the phase of a light wave stays predictable, and the coherence length Lc = cτc/n is how far it travels in that time. It sets the largest path difference an interferometer can have and still show high-contrast fringes, and it matters for fiber sensing, LIDAR, holography and the interference noise you get from stray reflections.

The number you get depends on the lineshape and on the definition. This page uses the Mandel–Wolf definition (the integral of |g⁽¹⁾(τ)|²), which gives τc = 1/(πΔν) for a Lorentzian line, typical of single-frequency lasers whose linewidth comes from phase noise, and √(2 ln 2/π)/Δν ≈ 0.664/Δν for a Gaussian line, typical of Doppler-broadened gas lasers or lasers dominated by slow frequency jitter. Many datasheets instead quote the rule of thumb Lc ≈ c/Δν = λ²/Δλ, which is about π times longer than the Lorentzian value, so check which convention a spec uses before comparing.

Inside a medium, use the group index, because the envelope of the wave travels at the group velocity. The visibility output is the magnitude of the first-order coherence |g⁽¹⁾| at a path difference ΔL, valid for a single-mode laser with that lineshape. Multimode lasers, such as Fabry-Pérot diode lasers, show visibility revivals at multiples of twice the cavity length, which this model does not include. Laser linewidths also depend on the measurement time, so a 100 kHz linewidth measured over 1 ms can broaden over seconds.

FAQ

What is the coherence length of a 1 MHz laser?

With a Lorentzian line, the coherence length = c/(πΔν) ≈ 95 m in air. The rule of thumb c/Δν gives about 300 m.

How do I convert linewidth in nm to MHz?

Δν = cΔλ/λ². At 1550 nm, 1 pm is about 125 MHz and 1 nm is about 125 GHz. At 633 nm, 1 pm is about 750 MHz.

Why is the HeNe coherence length often quoted as 20–30 cm?

A typical HeNe laser runs on two or three longitudinal modes inside a ~1.5 GHz Doppler gain width, so the usable coherence is limited by the mode beating and the cavity length rather than by the ~kHz linewidth of a single mode. A single-frequency stabilized HeNe reaches hundreds of meters.

Should I use the phase index or the group index?

The group index, since coherence is a property of the wave envelope. For standard single-mode fiber at 1550 nm it is about 1.468.
