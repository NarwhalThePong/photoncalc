Formula

z_R = π w₀² / (M² λ)    θ = M² λ / (π w₀)
w(z) = w₀ · √(1 + (z / z_R)²)    R(z) = z · [1 + (z_R / z)²]
Gouy phase ψ = arctan(z / z_R)    BPP = w₀ θ = M² λ / π

Worked example

A 1064 nm beam focused to w₀ = 25 µm with M² = 1: z_R = π (25 µm)² / 1.064 µm = 1.845 mm and θ = 1.064 / (π·25) = 13.55 mrad. At z = 10 mm the radius is 25·√(1 + (10/1.845)²) = 137.8 µm.

How it works

Almost every laser beam can be described as a Gaussian beam: it narrows to a waist w₀, stays roughly that size for a Rayleigh range z_R on either side, then spreads at a constant angle θ. The product w₀θ (the beam parameter product) cannot be reduced by any lens, which is why M² is the standard measure of beam quality (ISO 11146).

All radii are 1/e² intensity radii. Use M² = 1 for an ideal single-mode beam; real diode or multimode beams can have M² from 1.1 to well over 10.

FAQ

Is w₀ a radius or a diameter?

A radius, measured to the 1/e² (13.5 %) intensity point. Divide a quoted beam diameter by 2.

Half-angle or full-angle divergence?

Both are shown. Datasheets often quote the full angle 2θ.

What is the smallest BPP possible?

λ/π for M² = 1, about 0.339 mm·mrad at 1064 nm.
