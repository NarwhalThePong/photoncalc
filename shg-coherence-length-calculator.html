Formula

Δk = k(2ω) − 2k(ω) = (4π / λ_p) · [n(λ_p/2) − n(λ_p)]
L_c = π / |Δk| = λ_p / (4 |n(2ω) − n(ω)|)    first-order QPM period Λ = 2 L_c
P_SH(L) / P_SH(phase matched) = sinc²(Δk L / 2)

Worked example

5% MgO:LN (extraordinary, 24.5 °C) pumped at 1064 nm: n(1064) = 2.1482 and n(532) = 2.2244, so L_c = 1.064 µm / (4 × 0.0763) = 3.49 µm and the first-order poling period is 6.97 µm, matching the PPLN poling period calculator.

How it works

In second-harmonic generation the pump and its harmonic travel at different phase velocities because of dispersion. After one coherence length L_c they have slipped by π, and the harmonic generated further on starts cancelling what was made before. Without phase matching the harmonic power just oscillates with period 2L_c instead of growing.

Birefringent phase matching removes Δk with crystal orientation; quasi-phase matching flips the sign of χ⁽²⁾ every L_c (period Λ = 2L_c), which is how PPLN and poled fibers work. Glass and silicon have no bulk χ⁽²⁾, but their coherence lengths matter for poled fiber, strained silicon and surface SHG.

Indices come from the same Sellmeier fits as the Sellmeier calculator on this site, at room temperature; both λ_p and λ_p/2 must lie in the fit's range.

FAQ

Is coherence length the same as the poling period?

No. The first-order poling period is twice the coherence length, Λ = 2L_c.

Why is the coherence length so short in lithium niobate?

Its index rises steeply toward the UV, so n(532) and n(1064) differ by about 0.08 and the waves dephase within a few micrometres.

Does this include birefringent phase matching?

No. It uses one index (one polarization) for both waves, as in type-0 QPM. Type-I or type-II birefringent matching needs ordinary and extraordinary indices and an angle.
