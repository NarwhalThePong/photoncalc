Formula

Vertical step: TE₀ of the LN film on SiO₂ gives N_ridge (thickness t) and N_side (thickness t − etch)
Lateral step: TM₀ of a slab of width w, core N_ridge, cladding N_side, gives n_eff
Λ = m · λ_p / [2 (n_eff(λ_p/2) − n_eff(λ_p))]    bulk value: Λ = m / (n(λ_p/2)/(λ_p/2) − 2 n(λ_p)/λ_p)

Worked example

X-cut congruent LN film 600 nm thick, 300 nm etch, 1.4 µm top width, air cladding, 1550 nm pump at 25 °C: n_eff ≈ 1.913 at 1550 nm and 2.103 at 775 nm, so Λ ≈ 1.55 / (2 × 0.191) ≈ 4.06 µm. Published 600 nm-film PPLN waveguides for 1550 nm SHG use periods close to 4 µm, while bulk PPLN needs about 19 µm.

How it works

In a sub-micron lithium niobate waveguide the effective index of the guided mode is well below the bulk index, and much more so at the pump than at the second harmonic, because the longer wavelength spreads further into the silica and air. That geometric dispersion adds to the material dispersion, so TFLN poling periods are about 4–5 µm for 1550 nm SHG instead of the roughly 19 µm used in bulk PPLN.

This page uses the effective index method: it solves the vertical slab (film on oxide) inside and outside the ridge, then a lateral slab of the ridge width. The method treats sidewalls as vertical and ignores the real sidewall angle and mode hybridization, so expect an error of a few percent in the period. That is fine for planning a poling mask with a sweep of periods around the estimate, but final designs should use a full mode solver (FDE/FEM) with your measured film thickness and etch profile.

Both waves are the fundamental TE mode in an x-cut film (type-0, extraordinary polarization, using d₃₃). The substrate is SiO₂ (Malitson). Film-thickness variation of only ±10 nm shifts the period noticeably, which is why fabricated chips often carry several periods.

FAQ

Why is the TFLN period so much shorter than bulk PPLN?

Waveguide dispersion: the pump mode has a much lower effective index than the second-harmonic mode, so the phase mismatch grows and the period shrinks from about 19 µm to about 4 µm at 1550 nm.

How accurate is the effective index method here?

Typically within a few percent of a full vectorial mode solver for shallow-to-medium etches. It ignores sidewall angle, so use it to choose a period sweep, not a single final value.

Does this work for z-cut films?

Not directly. Z-cut uses the TM mode to access n_e, which needs the TM slab equations in the vertical step.
