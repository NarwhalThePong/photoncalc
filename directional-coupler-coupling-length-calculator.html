Worked example

Take illustrative supermode indices neven = 2.40 and nodd = 2.36 at 1550 nm (get real values for your waveguides from a mode solver). Then Δn = 0.04, so the full-transfer length is 1.55 µm / 0.08 ≈ 19.4 µm and κ ≈ 0.0811 µm⁻¹. A 10 µm coupling section gives κL ≈ 0.811, so sin²(0.811) ≈ 52.5 % goes to the cross port and 47.5 % stays in the bar port. A 50:50 split needs about 9.69 µm, and a 10 % tap needs arcsin(√0.1)/κ ≈ 3.97 µm.

How it works

A directional coupler is two waveguides run close enough that their evanescent fields overlap. The cleanest way to describe it is with the two supermodes of the coupled pair: an even (symmetric) mode and an odd (antisymmetric) mode with slightly different effective indices. Light launched into one waveguide excites both supermodes equally, and as they drift out of phase the power beats back and forth between the guides. After the beat length Lc = λ/(2Δn) the supermodes are π out of phase and all the power has crossed over.

This is equivalent to coupled-mode theory for two identical, phase-matched waveguides, with coupling coefficient κ = πΔn/λ: the cross-port power is sin²(κL) and the bar-port power is cos²(κL). The response is periodic, so lengths of Lc/2, 3Lc/2 and so on all give 50:50; the calculator reports the shortest.

Limits: the model assumes identical, lossless waveguides and counts only the straight coupling section. Real couplers also couple in the bends that bring the guides together, which acts like a little extra length, so measured or simulated designs are often a few micrometres shorter than this estimate. Δn depends strongly on the gap, wavelength and polarization, so a coupler designed at 1550 nm will not split the same way at 1310 nm.

FAQ

What is the coupling length of a directional coupler?

Usually the full-transfer (cross-over) length L_c = λ/(2Δn), where Δn is the difference between the even and odd supermode effective indices. Some texts call L_c/2, the 3 dB length, the coupling length instead, so check which one a paper means.

How long should a 50:50 directional coupler be?

Half the full-transfer length, L_c/2 = λ/(4Δn), minus whatever coupling the input and output bends contribute.

How do I design a 1 % or 10 % tap coupler?

Solve sin²(κL) = r for L, which gives L = arcsin(√r)/κ. A 10 % tap needs about 0.205 L_c and a 1 % tap about 0.064 L_c.

Where do the supermode indices come from?

From a 2D mode solver run on the cross-section of both waveguides together, for example an eigenmode or finite-difference solver. Δn shrinks roughly exponentially as the gap grows.
