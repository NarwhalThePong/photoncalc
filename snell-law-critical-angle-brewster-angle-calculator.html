Formula

n₁ sin θᵢ = n₂ sin θₜ    θ_c = arcsin(n₂ / n₁) (n₁ > n₂)    θ_B = arctan(n₂ / n₁)
r_s = (n₁cosθᵢ − n₂cosθₜ)/(n₁cosθᵢ + n₂cosθₜ)    r_p = (n₂cosθᵢ − n₁cosθₜ)/(n₂cosθᵢ + n₁cosθₜ)    R = r²

Worked example

Air to glass (n = 1.5) at 30°: θₜ = arcsin(sin 30° / 1.5) = 19.47°. Brewster's angle is arctan(1.5) = 56.31°. Going the other way, glass to air has a critical angle of arcsin(1/1.5) = 41.81°.

How it works

Snell's law gives the direction of the refracted ray. When light goes from a denser to a less dense medium beyond the critical angle there is no refracted ray at all: total internal reflection, which is how fibers and prisms guide light. At Brewster's angle p-polarized light is transmitted with zero reflection, used in laser windows and polarizers.

The Fresnel equations here are for lossless, non-magnetic, isotropic media.

FAQ

What is the reflectance of glass at normal incidence?

About 4 % per surface for n = 1.5: ((1.5−1)/(1.5+1))² = 0.04.

Is there a critical angle from air into glass?

No, total internal reflection needs n₁ > n₂.

Which polarization vanishes at Brewster's angle?

p (TM) polarization.
