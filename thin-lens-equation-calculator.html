Formula

1/f = 1/dₒ + 1/dᵢ  ⇒  dᵢ = 1 / (1/f − 1/dₒ)
M = −dᵢ / dₒ    lens power P = 1/f (dioptres when f in metres)

Worked example

An f = 100 mm lens with an object 150 mm away: dᵢ = 1/(1/100 − 1/150) = 300 mm, M = −300/150 = −2. The image is real, inverted and twice the size.

How it works

The Gaussian thin-lens equation relates object distance, image distance and focal length using the real-is-positive sign convention: real objects and real images (on the far side of the lens) have positive distances, a diverging lens has negative f, and a negative dᵢ means a virtual image on the same side as the object.

It assumes a lens whose thickness is small compared with the distances involved and paraxial (small-angle) rays. For thick lenses or systems, measure distances from the principal planes.

FAQ

What happens when the object is at the focal point?

The image goes to infinity: the lens outputs a collimated beam.

Why is magnification negative?

A negative M means the image is inverted.

Can I use any length unit?

Yes, as long as f and dₒ use the same unit; the lens power line assumes mm.
