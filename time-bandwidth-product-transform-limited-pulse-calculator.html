Formula

Δν = c Δλ / λ²    TBP = Δν · Δt ≥ K  (K = 2 ln2/π ≈ 0.441 Gaussian, 0.315 sech²)
Δt_TL = K / Δν    Gaussian after GDD: Δt = Δt_TL √(1 + (4 ln2 · GDD / Δt_TL²)²)

Worked example

A 100 fs pulse with 10 nm bandwidth at 800 nm: Δν = c × 10 nm / (800 nm)² = 4.68 THz, so the TBP is 0.468, about 6 % above the Gaussian limit of 0.441 (the transform-limited duration is 94.2 fs). Adding 1000 fs² of GDD stretches that transform-limited pulse to 98.7 fs.

How it works

A pulse cannot be both arbitrarily short and spectrally narrow: the product of its duration and bandwidth has a minimum set by its shape. A pulse sitting at that minimum is transform-limited (no chirp). Comparing your measured duration with the transform limit tells you how much residual chirp is left to compress.

Dispersion from glass, lenses and fiber adds group-delay dispersion (GDD), which chirps the pulse and stretches it. Shorter pulses are hit far harder because the broadening term scales as 1/Δt². For material GDD, multiply the GVD from the Sellmeier calculator by the thickness.

FAQ

Which TBP value should I use?

0.441 for Gaussian pulses and 0.315 for sech² pulses, both with FWHM intensity widths in time and frequency. Mode-locked solitons are usually sech².

Is my autocorrelation width the pulse width?

No, divide the intensity-autocorrelation FWHM by 1.414 (Gaussian) or 1.543 (sech²) first.

How much GDD does 1 cm of glass add?

Fused silica adds about 36 fs²/mm at 800 nm, so roughly 360 fs² per cm.
