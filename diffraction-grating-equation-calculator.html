Formula

m λ = d (sin α + sin β),   d = 1 / (groove density)
dispersion dβ/dλ = m / (d cos β)    Littrow (α = β): sin θ_L = m λ / (2d)

Worked example

600 lines/mm (d = 1.667 µm), 500 nm at normal incidence, first order: sin β = 0.5/1.667 = 0.3, β = 17.46°. A 1200 lines/mm grating at 1064 nm has a first-order Littrow angle of arcsin(1.064 × 1.2 / 2) = 39.67°.

How it works

A grating sends each wavelength to a different angle, which is the basis of spectrometers, monochromators and external-cavity tunable lasers. Angles are measured from the grating normal and are positive on the same side as the incident beam (the common reflection-grating convention), so m = −1 is the order on the other side.

An order exists only if |sin β| ≤ 1; the calculator flags orders that cannot propagate.

FAQ

What is the Littrow configuration?

The diffracted beam returns along the incident beam (α = β), used in tunable external-cavity diode lasers.

Transmission or reflection grating?

The same equation applies; only the side the light exits changes.

How do I get resolving power?

R = mN, where N is the number of illuminated grooves.
