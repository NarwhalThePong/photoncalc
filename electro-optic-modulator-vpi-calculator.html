Formula

Δφ = π n³ r Γ V L / (λ g)   ⇒   Vπ (phase modulator) = λ g / (n³ r Γ L)
Push-pull MZM: Vπ = λ g / (2 n³ r Γ L)    Figure of merit: Vπ·L (V·cm)    phase per volt = π / Vπ

Worked example

Thin-film LN at 1550 nm (n_e ≈ 2.138, r₃₃ = 30.9 pm/V, so n³r ≈ 302 pm/V), 5 µm electrode gap, Γ = 0.5, 10 mm electrodes: a single-arm phase modulator needs Vπ = 1.55 µm × 5 µm / (302 pm/V × 0.5 × 10 mm) ≈ 5.13 V, i.e. Vπ·L ≈ 5.1 V·cm. Driven push-pull as a Mach-Zehnder, Vπ ≈ 2.57 V and Vπ·L ≈ 2.6 V·cm, about 0.61 rad/V. Published TFLN Mach-Zehnder modulators report about 2.2–2.8 V·cm with gaps near 5 µm, so the estimate lands in the right range.

How it works

The Pockels effect changes the refractive index in proportion to the applied field: Δn = ½ n³ r E. With electrodes a gap g apart, the field in the waveguide is roughly Γ·V/g, where the overlap factor Γ (between 0 and 1) accounts for how much of the optical mode sees the full field. Over an electrode length L the optical phase changes by Δφ = (2π/λ) Δn L = π n³ r Γ V L / (λ g). Setting Δφ = π gives the half-wave voltage Vπ = λ g / (n³ r Γ L). Because Vπ scales as 1/L, the length-independent figure of merit is Vπ·L, usually quoted in V·cm (lower is better).

In a Mach-Zehnder modulator, switching from full transmission to extinction needs a π difference between the two arms. With a single driven arm that is the same voltage as a phase modulator. In the usual push-pull layout (a ground-signal-ground electrode on x-cut LN, or series push-pull), the two arms get opposite field directions, so each needs only π/2 and Vπ is halved. Datasheets and papers almost always quote the push-pull Vπ·L for MZMs, so compare like with like.

Assumptions: the lithium niobate presets use the extraordinary index of congruent LN from the Jundt (1997) Sellmeier equation at 25 °C (2.138 at 1550 nm) and r₃₃ = 30.9 pm/V, the value commonly used in TFLN modulator papers, for TE light in x-cut film (or TM in z-cut bulk) so that the field is along the crystal z axis. r is not scaled with wavelength. The electric field is treated as uniform V/g scaled by Γ, which is a lumped approximation: Γ ≈ 0.4–0.6 for TFLN ridges with electrodes beside the ridge and ≈ 0.3–0.5 for bulk Ti-diffused or proton-exchanged LN waveguides, whose gaps are wider (≈ 10–20 µm). This is a DC/low-frequency estimate. RF velocity mismatch, electrode loss and the bandwidth–voltage trade-off of traveling-wave electrodes are not included, and the clamped (high-frequency) r₃₃ is a few percent lower. Use a mode solver plus an electrostatic solver to get Γ for a real cross-section.

FAQ

What Vπ·L do thin-film lithium niobate modulators reach?

Push-pull TFLN Mach-Zehnder modulators with about 5 µm gaps typically report 2–3 V·cm at 1550 nm (for example about 2.2 V·cm in Wang et al., Nature 2018), versus roughly 10–20 V·cm for conventional bulk LN modulators, because the tight mode allows a much smaller gap at the same Γ.

Why does push-pull halve Vπ?

Each arm is driven with the opposite field, so one arm gains +π/2 while the other gains −π/2. Their difference reaches π at half the voltage a single arm would need.

What overlap factor Γ should I use?

Γ is the normalized overlap between the optical intensity and the applied field. About 0.5 is a fair starting point for TFLN ridge waveguides with side electrodes, and 0.3–0.5 for bulk LN waveguides. Compute it with an electrostatic plus optical mode simulation for a final design.
