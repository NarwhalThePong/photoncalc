Formula

η = [2 w₁w₂ / (w₁² + w₂²)]² · exp[−2d² / (w₁² + w₂²)] · exp[−2π² n² θ² w₁² w₂² / (λ² (w₁² + w₂²))]
loss = −10 log₁₀ η

Worked example

Two SMF-28 fibers (MFD 10.4 µm at 1550 nm, w = 5.2 µm) with a 1 µm lateral offset: η = exp(−1² / 5.2²) = 96.4 %, a 0.16 dB loss. Butting SMF-28 against a 4 µm MFD waveguide mode (w = 2 µm) loses 3.48 dB from mode size alone.

How it works

When two single-mode beams or waveguides are joined, the power transferred is the overlap integral of their mode fields. Approximating both modes as Gaussians gives closed-form factors for size mismatch, lateral offset and tilt, which multiply. This is the standard way to budget splice, connector and edge-coupler loss.

The model ignores Fresnel reflection at a glass–air gap (about 0.15 dB per pair of uncoated faces; use index-matching gel to remove it) and longitudinal gaps, and assumes both modes are well described by Gaussians.

FAQ

What is a typical splice loss?

Fusion splices between identical single-mode fibers are often below 0.05 dB; the lateral-offset term shows why sub-micron alignment matters.

Where do I get the mode radius?

Half of the mode-field diameter (MFD) from the fiber data sheet, or from a mode solver for waveguides.

Why does tilt hurt more for big modes?

A large mode has a small far-field divergence, so a small angular error moves it well outside the other mode's acceptance.
